# Classroom demonstration

Current revision, 11 October 2026: demonstrate the 2020/2024 selector and unknown historical populations; the frozen Taal protocol, rejected wind coverage and both dependent reference contours; and explicit graphics recovery. These examples demonstrate software/evidence handling, not established historical agreement. Read [GAP_REGISTER.md](GAP_REGISTER.md) for remaining source and QA limits.

Start the installed application with `./scripts/start.ps1`, open http://127.0.0.1:8000 and keep the academic/illustrative labels visible.

## Added eruption walkthrough

1. Run Taal, beginning paused at Before. Select During to inspect source billows, wind transport and the schematic fountain. Select After: source emission ends at 60 min but fine ash remains aloft. Move the slider to its end to show eventual deposition at about 1,674.6 min under the assumed defaults. Explain the nonuniform accelerated clock and flat-ground release assumption.
2. Run Mayon. Open Eruption illustration & lava to identify assumed effusion, viscosity, width and density. Choose Lava channel in Map effect, then Channel extent. Seek Before/During/After to compare the advancing 3D front with its current-time map footprint. The widened 3D ribbon/centerline and stage colors aid readability; the buffered polygon uses the prescribed width. A stopped route does not establish real runout.
3. Open Effects, reference zones & sources and enable the dated 6 km Mayon reference. Explain that this PHIVOLCS multi-hazard reference is from 24 November 2023, distinct from computed ash, computed channel width and today's alerts. Taal uses island context and Kanlaon a dated 4 km reference, not invented ash radii.
4. Increase modeled ash rate to 1,000,000 kg/s as an explicitly custom teaching sensitivity case; Run, seek the end and choose Accumulated ash. Inspect a colored receptor and compare its physical thickness/load with the eventual table. This is not a historical eruption configuration. Restore the preset afterward.

## Existing comparison and evidence walkthrough

1. Choose Taal teaching scenario and Run. Explain Q×T, FROM 225 degrees, the eruption column, representative class paths and eventual deposit. Open grain controls to show five fractions summing to one. Play, pause, reset and choose Regional overview from Camera view. Model limits are in the header's Academic model disclosure.
2. Use the adjacent Ground deposition map (stacked below 3D on narrow screens). Explain its thickness legend and surface load; Deposit extent fits computed contours. Select a map point or use Inspect location; explain the centroid, 2024 population year, outside coverage and unknown damage criteria. Expand Location table & exports only when needed.
3. Change wind direction without running. The previous map/table remain visible with a stale warning. Run to update all shared views. Switch to Mayon and Kanlaon to compare source-traced geographic forms and receptor coverage. Open Terrain source & graphics to explain the EGM96 elevation grid, reference-height differences and the lack of terrain physics.
4. In Wind comparison, explain why reviewed Amihan/Habagat winds are unavailable. Enter manual winds 10 m/s FROM 45 and FROM 225 and compute both. Eruption parameters are held fixed; this is a user-defined comparison, not measured seasonal weather.
5. Run Wind sweep. Explain each municipal maximum can occur under a different direction. Select a location and its displayed maximizing direction, then Run. Inspect that direction's complete result. Roof/crop statuses remain unknown. Export CSV and scenario JSON. In Screening & population, select 2020 and run again; missing census counts remain unknown. Edit the year without running and verify the exported completed request retains its original year.
6. Open Taal 2020 and Check historical reference. Explain the frozen mapped interval, ASL height conversion, 71.43% rejected sounding coverage and preserved Table 3/narrative footprint discrepancy. Open provenance and inspect both independently digitized contours. They share one source and are not model predictions. Unavailable evaluation is a scientific evidence gap, not a successful replication.
7. Open Guide & sources and the two Markdown guides. Derive falling time, wind transport, diffusion variance and load-to-thickness conversion with the tested synthetic worked example.
8. With no MapTiler key, all assets/scientific inputs are local. Demonstrate coastline, a new computation, table and exports without an external tile service. Installation itself requires downloaded dependencies.

The animation is an accelerated illustration of representative paths, not eruption duration or an airborne concentration forecast. Ground screening establishes neither structural safety nor drinking-water/flight safety. Foreground GPU performance and historical agreement remain separate evidence requirements.
