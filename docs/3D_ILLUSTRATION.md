The source-column bend is a bounded illustration cue using the Python result's wind vector and the prescribed rise-speed setting; it does not solve buoyancy, turbulence or gas transport. Cloud texture and opacity are not airborne concentration.

`eruptionVisuals.ts` owns procedural texture, deterministic display detail, sparse grain markers, and the surface lava ribbon. The ribbon samples the same triangular terrain mesh as the backdrop, including reduced detail, so its enlarged sides lie above the displayed surface. Its centerline and advancing front still come from Python's route and arrival times. It has a dark edge and warmer core; these are qualitative stage colors, not computed temperatures. Mayon's camera is oblique to the routed slope to reveal downhill length. Taal's framing includes more of the source-column top.

The current [visual review](3D_VISUAL_REVISION.md) records browser evidence and remaining gates. Independent final review, full graphics-cleanup retest and empirical validation remain open.

The React Three Fiber v9 / Three.js view presents local geographic terrain alongside the result map. It renders backend results; JavaScript does not solve ash transport, plume dynamics or lava routing. ## Shared before / during / after timeline

The eruption-sequence workflow adds a finite-duration teaching timeline to the existing eventual-deposition solver. `Workspace.tsx` owns Play, Pause, Replay and the selected frame; both map and scene read that frame. The scene's independent settling controls disappear when a sequence is supplied. The clock is piecewise compressed for presentation, not a uniformly accelerated record of an observed eruption.

- **Before:** clear terrain, no source column, ash cloud, fountain or lava deposit.
- **Erupting:** Python's `column_top_m` controls the rising source; its plume parcels supply projected centers, horizontal spreading radii and grain identities. White source billows illustrate condensed steam in a gas/ash plume, while gray overlapping billows make the ash cloud visible. They are not gas chemistry, concentration isosurfaces or CFD. Fountain paths and hot-fragment positions come from Python; Taal's illustrative fountain does not imply a broad downslope lava flow.
- **Settling:** the source and condensation stem stop. Detached Python ash parcels continue drifting/settling; the map and optional detached overlay use the actual frame's cumulative deposition grid and contours.
- **After:** the accumulated ash remains on the map. An enabled lava channel retains its reached footprint in a dark cooling color; the color is schematic and predicts no temperature or solidification time.

`eruptionReplay.ts` matches adjacent frame parcels by stable `parcel_id` and interpolates their coordinates and radii. These are equal-mass cloud quantile centers, not individual particle histories. Entering or departing clouds fade by opacity; sparse grain markers fade by display size. This is presentation interpolation, not an extra mass calculation. Backend frame diagnostics and deposition remain authoritative. Coarse/fine settling, source duration, wind and spreading therefore come from Python; changing a visual opacity never changes a scientific value.

`EruptionEffects.tsx` batches at most 256 ash-cloud centers (96 in reduced detail), with three soft sprites per center (two in reduced detail), into one instanced mesh. Horizontal cloud size follows the supplied spread radius; vertical shape, overlap, opacity and the source-column condensation cue are artistic. Up to 240 sparse ash markers (96 in reduced detail) use stable cohort IDs and submitted grain diameters for display size. Marker offsets around cohort centers are schematic, not extra random particle trajectories. Gas itself is not drawn as a measured visible substance. A zero ash mass rate renders no ash, steam stem or fountain.

The source fountain is enlarged 4× in the scene so its arcs remain visible beside the condensation stem; fragment radii therefore display at 100 m. Backend fountain coordinates, flight time and physical assumptions are unchanged. This display enlargement is explained in Illustration guide and does not create a ballistic map footprint or match observed Taal fountain height.

Lava coordinates arrive as absolute EGM96 terrain elevations, unlike ash heights above the flat vent datum. The renderer does **not** add vent elevation to lava. `lavaVisiblePath` clips the route at Python `arrival_s` values and linearly interpolates its moving front. The surface ribbon resamples that same centerline and samples the displayed terrain at its enlarged sides; its display width is at least 130 m. A 4 m display offset plus the supplied thickness avoids z-fighting. The oblique close-up reveals the routed slope. The enlarged surface ribbon is not the quantitative footprint and does not add terrain routing or collision physics. Raw terrain, physical coordinates, arrival times and the map polygon are unchanged.

The qualitative column shape is informed by USGS descriptions of a buoyant eruption column and spreading umbrella ([Pinatubo column study](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pinatubo/koya/)) and ash formation/transport ([USGS volcanic ash](https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/msh/ash.html)). These are visual/physical references, not validation of ABO's simplified plume or lava models. Backend equations, assumptions and numerical evidence remain in `COMPUTATION.md` and `VALIDATION.md`.

## Quantity-to-scene mapping

- Backend east/north/height metres become world `(east / 1000, (height + submitted_vent_elevation_m) / 1000, -north / 1000)` when geographic terrain is active. World units are kilometres; the uniform vertical translation makes the flat model datum explicit in the ASL terrain scene. No terrain collision, snapping or path bending occurs. The world compass marks north and east and rotates with the scene.
- The eruption column uses submitted height above the flat model-ground datum. Its width is schematic and does not represent a computed plume cross-section or airborne concentration.
- A thin dashed connector marks any visible gap between the sampled DEM surface at the vent and the submitted source-elevation datum. This is especially relevant to Taal, whose registered Main Crater Lake coordinate has a lower sampled surface than the volcano-profile elevation. The connector explains the diagram offset; it does not move or bend backend paths. The guide states both heights.
- Representative trajectories arrive as sampled Python `(east, north, height)` coordinates. React linearly interpolates adjacent samples for animation. Five classes are presented as distinct colored paths and matching particles when the scenario contains five classes. The class legend also gives diameter, submitted settling speed, and backend representative falling time, so class interpretation does not rely on color alone. No JavaScript settling solver is present.
- The replay uses one shared physical clock, from zero to the longest Python-provided `settling_time_s`, compressed into 20 display seconds. Each class is interpolated at `min(physical_elapsed / settling_time_s, 1)`: faster classes reach their endpoints earlier and remain there while slower classes continue. The visible clock states the time compression. No class-specific launch delays are used. Particle radius and tiny marker separation are enlarged for visibility and are not a computed dispersion envelope or ash concentration.
- The geographic map carries quantitative accumulated deposition at the selected sequence snapshot, or eventual deposition when inspecting a standalone comparison result. An optional, initially hidden detached texture layer uses actual cell-average deposit thickness with fixed levels 0.5, 1, 5, 10, 50, and 100 mm. Its horizontal extent matches the model domain; colors encode thickness rather than hazard severity. It floats above the highest local terrain for visibility, with the exact display height disclosed. This is accumulated ground deposition, never airborne concentration; cells below 0.5 mm are transparent.
- Municipal/facility markers are small ground-point discs using the same projected receptor coordinates and selection identifier as the table; they are not airborne spheres or municipality-sized areas. Selected points enlarge only as a selection cue. The geographic view uses backend geographic contours and receptor coordinates.

## Geographic terrain and volcano forms

The normal scene uses manually prepared static assets `/terrain/taal.json`, `/terrain/mayon.json`, and `/terrain/kanlaon.json`. Each contains a vent-centered AEQD heightfield sampled every 250 m over 100 × 100 km, with 401 east coordinates and 401 north coordinates. Elevations are in metres EGM96 from Mapzen Skadi/SRTM; land/water classification and the precise source details accompany the assets. Regional overview, Mayon and Kanlaon use true horizontal and vertical scales. **Taal's close-up deliberately enlarges Volcano Island relief six times** so its low island and crater remain legible beside the eruption column. A 500 m display mesh is available in reduced-detail mode. Terrain is display context, not a new numerical input to the flat-ground solver.

For Taal close-up only, land within 3 km of the registered vent displays as `3 + 6 × (source_elevation_m − 3)` above the historical 3 m lake surface. A smooth cubic taper returns the multiplier to one between 3 and 5 km, keeping the surrounding caldera unaltered. Water vertices, horizontal positions, lake/island holes and source assets are unchanged. The visible label reads **Island relief ×6 · horizontal 1:1**; selecting Regional overview restores original relief. `terrainVertexHeightM` in `frontend/src/components/terrain.ts` supplies the same display transform for mesh vertices, ground markers, the datum connector and the optional detached layer's clearance. The backend paths, submitted source elevation and column height retain their original quantities. An enlarged ridge may visually occlude a flat-model path; this is not terrain interception.

Sea vertices render at 0 m; Lake Taal vertices render at the documented historical illustration level of 3 m. Other lake levels are unspecified schematic levels, not surveyed elevations. Land uses sampled DEM elevations. Taal Lake uses cached OpenStreetMap relation 270582 and its seven native island holes; no elevation threshold invents its island outline. No generic cone is placed over the real heightfield. Taal's caldera/lake/island, Mayon's cone and Albay Gulf context, and Kanlaon's irregular mountain form therefore come from the local geographic data. Fine crater detail below the 250 m display sampling is not reconstructed or claimed. The rendered view carries Mapzen/USGS/NOAA, Natural Earth and OpenStreetMap ODbL attribution; see `GEOGRAPHY_REFERENCES.md` for exact sources and discrepancies.

The source-profile vent elevation is metadata, not necessarily the DEM surface at the exact vent coordinate. The model column and representative paths translate by the submitted vent elevation; differences from the source-profile reference are disclosed. Ground receptor discs sample the display surface solely to avoid appearing airborne. Geographic coverage is 100 × 100 km even when the model domain is 200 × 200 km; terrain outside coverage remains neutral and unknown. The adjacent map/table retain full model receptor coverage.

Assets are accepted only at their registered volcano and exact origin. Editing vent latitude/longitude hides the fixed terrain rather than moving real geography. Loading, missing, malformed or mismatched assets have a visible status and use an explicitly labeled schematic fallback; source details preserve the specific failure. Only that fallback uses procedural Taal lake/caldera, Mayon cone and Kanlaon shoulder forms informed by the Smithsonian GVP profiles; these fallback shapes are not surveyed terrain.

## Controls and accessibility

The opening camera is a column-height-adaptive volcano close-up. Taal uses a closer, higher oblique camera to reveal its crater while including the column; other volcanoes keep their existing framing. Regional overview, close-up, reset, orbit and zoom operate real cameras. Focus the canvas: arrow keys orbit/raise the camera and plus/minus zoom. The geographic map and keyboard-accessible exposure table remain alternative views of the same completed result. Parameter edits mark the completed result stale without overwriting it.

The scene identifies Taal/Volcano Island (Lake Taal in regional overview), Mayon/Albay Gulf, or Kanlaon/Negros and states the applicable scale or relief enlargement. The compass is sized and positioned for the close-up or regional camera extent; its arrows remain world north/east during orbit. Compass letters use opaque white backplates and dark blue sRGB text; their material bypasses fog and tone mapping so navigation annotations retain contrast against terrain. This changes annotation presentation, not geographic coordinates. A smaller downwind arrow indicates transport direction only: its 2 km close-up or 8 km regional length is an illustration size, not a wind-speed scale.

Replay pauses when the page is hidden or reduced-motion preference becomes active. Explicit Play remains available as a user-controlled educational illustration. Switching view unmounts the scene and stops playback. Initial WebGL 2 failure or subsequent context loss stops playback and exposes the usable map/table alternative; camera and playback controls are disabled in that state.

## Resources and performance

Particles use one instanced mesh with 24 representatives per class; receptor markers also share one instanced mesh. Paused scenes render on demand. Pixel density is capped at 1.5 and lowered to 1 when measured playback drops below 30 fps. A reduced-detail control selects density 1 and eight particles per class. The UI reports measured replay frame rate over recent two-second render windows, with browser animation-callback pacing available separately to diagnose scheduling limits. Measurements exclude paused idle time. This is browser/hardware evidence, not a universal performance promise.

Orbit controls, event handlers, textures, custom line materials/geometries, arrow helpers and compass sprites are disposed on unmount. React Three Fiber disposes declarative geometry/material resources. The 3D renderer is a lazy-loaded chunk; its size is primarily Three.js and Fiber rather than initial workspace code.
